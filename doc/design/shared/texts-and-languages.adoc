[#two-languages]
== Two Languages

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Setting |Governs |Never governs

|Interface language
|every text shown to the operator: the web app, the prompts and messages of `pm-operator` and `pm-mcp`, the explanations and facts of a state
|command names, options, identifiers, state ids, store keys, log messages

|Model language
|the language a model is told to write in and the language of the content it is given (task content, questions, answers, summaries)
|the instructions of the units and roles, which stay in English
|===

The few texts of `pm-mcp` (the relay's errors, which the harness and its model read) follow the interface language like those of `pm-operator` (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/01-onboarding.adoc#UC-ONB-3[UC-ONB-3]); their keys live in the area `relay`.

A question a model asks the operator is in the model language; the frame around it (buttons, what it blocks, the recommendation label) is in the interface language.

[#style]
== How Texts Are Written

* *Say what happens.* A button names its action ("Look for harnesses"), a message names what happened and what to do next. No "OK", "Next", "Submit".
* *Short.* A title is one line; the explanation under it at most two sentences; detail goes behind _Why?_.
* *No blame, no alarm.* An error says what failed, the answer received, and the fix; a "no" by the operator is never shown as an error.
* *The same text in both clients.* A text is written once and used by the web app and the terminal; a text that only one client needs has a key only that client uses.
* *German addresses the operator with _du_*, throughout and in every client; the imperative of a button follows it ("Suche nach Harnesses", "Prüfe erneut").
* *Arguments, not concatenation.* Every variable part is an argument of the message (`{harness}`, `{count, plural, one {# role} other {# roles}}`), so that a translation can reorder it.

[#acts-and-links]
=== Acts and Links

* *An act is an imperative with its object*, short, in sentence case ("Start the plan", "End the plan…"; German "Starte den Plan", "Beende den Plan…"); without an object only where the object is obvious ("Save", "Speichere"). *A link names its destination as a noun* ("Completion report", "Abschlussbericht"). A noun never labels an act, with one exception: the settings of a thing, which open in a dialogue, are named by their noun and `…` ("Plan settings…", "Plan-Einstellungen…"). A question never labels a button.
* *The acts that leave without acting* keep their one short word in German, the word every dialogue of the operating system uses: "Zurück", "Abbrechen", "Schließen", "Später". Every other act takes the _du_ imperative; _Retry_ is "Try again" / "Versuche es erneut" everywhere, and reconnecting is an act of its own.
* *`…`* ends the label of an act if and only if a dialogue follows that asks for more: every confirmation, a rename, an upgrade ("End the plan…", "Release the project…"). A link never ends with `…`.
* *One label, one act.* Two acts never share a label, and an act carries the same label in every view and in both clients. The end of a plan is "End the plan…" and the end of an epic "End the epic…", so "Cancel" / "Abbrechen" in a dialogue means only "close without acting".
* *No glyph as a label* (`↑ ↓ ↻ × +`): an icon with its accessible name from the catalogue. No literal: every word is a key.

[#field-texts]
=== Labels, Hints, Placeholders, Errors

* *A label names the value* as a noun phrase, in sentence case, without a colon and without an instruction ("Note", "Reason", "Path of the checkout"; never "Add a note"). An optional field says `(optional)` after its label (`common.field.optional`); a required field carries no mark.
* *A hint is one sentence* under the field, with the format or the consequence ("Moving deletes the files in the checkout."). It is never repeated in the placeholder.
* *A placeholder is only an example value* (`KXQB-MRTN`, `~/git/my-service`), never the label and never the hint.
* *A unit is a word beside the field* ("[30] days"), and a sentence is never split around a field: German word order does not survive it.
* *No machine id is a label*: an aspect, a recipe parameter or a setting has a catalogue name.
* *An error says what is wrong and what to do*, in one wording per rule ("Give a reason." for every missing reason, `common.field.reason_required`). It never is a raw exception message and never the label of a button.

[#keys]
== Keys and Packs

* *Key*: `<area>.<use case or view>.<state or element>[.<part>]`, at least three parts, for example `onboard.harness.choose.title`, `onboard.harness.choose.action.check`, `common.consent.no`. The area is the pack the key lives in. Four families are exempt, because both clients read them everywhere and none belongs to one view: `state.<kind>.<value>`, the display word of a server value; `unit.<id>`, a workflow unit's plain name (<<titles>>); and `phase.name.<phase>` and `role.name.<role>`, the word of a phase and of a role (<<phases>>). They live in `common`, their leading parts name a kind and a value rather than a view, a display word carries no argument because it is a tag, and a unit id with no dot of its own keeps the two-part key (`unit.epic`).
* *Areas*, each a pack; a client loads only the areas it shows, and the web app loads `common` and `web` at start and every other area when it first opens a view of it:
** `common`: texts every view uses, among them the texts of the consent states (link:consents.adoc#texts[Consents, Texts]) and of a setting changed meanwhile (link:work-actions-texts.adoc#texts-conflict[Changed Meanwhile]);
** `error`: the texts of every error code of the local API, a title, a cause and a fix per code, and the unknown fallback both clients show for a code they do not know;
** `onboard`: the onboarding of the machine and its later changes in the machine settings (`onboard.settings.*`, link:../terminal-client/23-texts-settings.adoc#texts-settings[Terminal Client, Texts: Machine Settings]);
** `web`: the frame and navigation of the web app;
** `plan`: the plan view (link:plan-view.adoc#texts[Plan View]);
** `decision`: what both clients show of a decision and a waiver request (link:decisions-texts.adoc#texts[Decisions, Texts]);
** `overview`: running work, ended work, and the epic view (link:plan-overview.adoc#texts[Plan Overview], link:epic-view.adoc#texts[Epic View]);
** `intent`: the plan's and the epic's intent, the builder check, the outline, and the `over_size` distribution (link:intent-and-outline-texts.adoc#texts[Intent and Outline, Texts]);
** `project`: the projects, their onboarding, release, and unenrolment (link:project-explorer.adoc#texts[Project Explorer], link:project-onboarding.adoc#texts[Project Onboarding]);
** `start`: starting a plan or an epic (link:starting-work-texts.adoc#texts[Starting Work, Texts], link:starting-work-sources-texts.adoc#texts[Starting Work, Texts of the Sources]);
** `control`: the actions on plans and epics, the holds, and the reviews (link:work-actions-texts.adoc#texts[Actions on Plans and Epics, Texts], link:work-actions-review-texts.adoc#texts[Actions on Plans and Epics, Texts of the Reviews]);
** `devices`: the paired devices (link:paired-devices.adoc#texts[Paired Devices]);
** `report`: the reports to the operator and a plan's completion report (link:reports.adoc#texts[Reports], link:completion-report.adoc#texts[Completion Report]);
** `system`: the system view, the acts on the runtime, and the cleanup (link:system-texts.adoc#texts[System, Texts], link:cleanup-texts.adoc#texts[Cleanup, Texts]);
** `designer`: the process designer, loaded by the web app only (link:../web-app/process-designer-texts.adoc#texts[Process Designer, Texts]);
** `operator`: the verbs of `pm-operator`;
** `relay`: `pm-mcp`.
* *Files*: `messages/<area>.<locale>.properties`, UTF-8, ICU message syntax (arguments, `plural` with `one`, `other`, exact `=n` cases and `#`, `select` with `other`), which `pm-api` evaluates itself; no formatting library is used. English (`en`) is complete and the base; German (`de`) ships from the start.
* *One word per value*: where an area pack still carries its own word for a value the shared `state.*` family names, the shared key is the authority. The area key stays only until the view that reads it moves to the shared key, and is removed then — except a variant that gives the state a point the chip cannot carry (`overview.state.pausing` names the task or the phase it stops after); that one stays an area key beside the shared word. A value has one word in each language: _held_ is "aufgehalten" in German wherever it stands, a state or a reason.
* *One key per concept*, in both clients (link:../web-app/components-tags.adoc#tag-words[Web App, Tags and Kinds, One Word per Value]); a second key for one of these is merged into the one named here and removed, in English and in German alike:
** *a state* through `state.*` only;
** *a reason* (what needs the operator) through one `overview.needs.<reason>` key per reason. Where the contract accepts two spellings of one reason, `pm-api` normalises them to the spelling of the runtime's closed list, as `NeedsOperatorKind.fromValue` already does (`waiver_request` to `waiver`, `operator_wait` to `waits_for_you`), and every reader of both clients takes the normalised value, so one key remains: `overview.needs.waiver` and `overview.needs.waits_for_you`. The kind name of the waiver is `waiver` everywhere a client names it — a badge, a notice, a notification, a row — and the parse alias `waiver_request` stays only where the contract is read. `overview.needs.waiver_request` is removed: no reader asks for it, the console's notification reading `overview.needs.waiver` like every other reason. `overview.needs.operator_wait` is removed once no reader asks for it;
** *a kind* through `common.kind.<kind>` (`project`, `epic`, `plan`, `side_job`, `decision`, `report`, `machine`), the only kind word of both clients: what an item blocks, the kind of a match, and the path of a machine's item read it;
** *a shared act* through one key each, in `common`: `common.action.back`, `common.action.close`, `common.action.cancel`, `common.action.save`, `common.action.retry`, `common.action.later` (the two-part `common.back`, `common.close` and `common.save`, the `web.common.*` copies and `common.actions.back` merge into them); the link to a plan's completion report through `report.completion.link`; a filter's label through `common.filter.label` with the list's name as its argument;
** *one act of one view* through one family: the acts of the decomposition through `decomposition.*`, whichever view shows it.
* *Build checks*: a pack that lacks a key of the English pack fails the build, as does a key that exists in a pack but not in English, an argument that differs from the English one, and a glossary term translated in a German text (checked against the glossary above).
* *Delivery*: the command-line binaries carry the packs as resources of `pm-api`; the runtime serves `GET /api/v1/messages/{locale}/{area}` with an entity tag, so a browser keeps a loaded pack until the release changes it, and `GET /api/v1/messages` with the locales that have a pack and their areas; both without a device secret, so the pairing page has its texts. The pack shown is that of the machine's interface language; the locale the client sends counts only while none is set. *Retention*: once the runtime version is known, a browser keeps one catalogue per locale, keyed by that version (`pm_i18n_messages_<locale>@<version>`), and a pack of another version is dropped rather than rendered. Before the version is known — the offline first paint — the newest cached pack of the locale is rendered, and the first write after the version is known replaces it. A pack that is missing is fetched from the runtime; no older catalogue is kept as a rollback copy — that is the operator's decision of 2026-11-05, because a read costs a request and no staleness.

[#phases]
== Phases and Roles

The six phases of a plan and the roles that ask are product terms: they keep their English word in both languages, as the glossary words do, and the catalogue owns them.

[cols="2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Key |English |German

|`phase.name.init` |init |init
|`phase.name.refine` |refine |refine
|`phase.name.outline` |outline |outline
|`phase.name.plan` |plan |plan
|`phase.name.execute` |execute |execute
|`phase.name.finalize` |finalize |finalize
|===

* *The word, not the token.* A client shows a phase through its `phase.name.<phase>` key, never the token the runtime sends, so the guard on texts finds no wire value on screen. A role the runtime names (the role that asks a decision) is shown through `role.name.<role>` the same way, its German value equal to its English one. `phase.<phase>` is the phase's icon id and is no text (link:../web-app/workflow-visual-language.adoc#mapping[Web App, Workflow Visual Language, Unit and Phase Map]).
* *Lowercase in both languages*, as the runtime's own words are: `execute`, never "Execute" or "Ausführen".
* *Never with its kind*: a phase's node is `init`, never `Phase init` or `Phase phase init`.

[#titles]
== Titles and Ids

A value is named by its title; the id is what the operator types, not what the operator reads.

* *The title first.* A plan, an epic, a project, a task, a step, and a workflow unit are named by their title in every view of both clients: a list, a detail, a report, a completion message, a prompt.
* *The id beside it.* The id follows the title, dim in the terminal and as a chip in the web app (`Fix Relay Transport  fix-relay`), and only where the operator types it or matches on it: a search, a filter, a command line, the detail an action is taken from. A view that never needs the id shows none, and no view shows the id to fill space.
* *Never the id alone.* A value without a title is a defect of the data, never a reason to fall back to the id. The display layer brings a build check that fails on a step id or a link name with no catalogue text, so a raw id cannot reach the operator once that layer lands.
* *Ended work.* Ended work follows the rule as running work does, with the same words for the same state.
* *A unit id.* A workflow unit id renders the plain name of its `unit.<id>` key: the id `landing.pull-request` reads "Pull request" through `unit.landing.pull-request`, with the id as a chip beside it where the operator needs it.

[#time]
== Time and Duration

Every instant and every duration is shown in one format, defined here and nowhere else, as <<numbers>> defines counts of tokens. The runtime sends the exact value — the instant, the span in seconds (`duration_seconds`) — and a client only formats it: the terminal client through `Display.time` and `Display.duration`, which `pm-api` gains with the display layer; the web app through its own display layer, which produces the same strings. One rule, one implementation per client, and no second way of writing a time in either once the display layer replaces the inline formatters.

|`25 min`
|`25 min`

|Duration under 2 d
|`2 h 10 min`
|`2 h 10 min`

|Duration of 2 d or more
|`9 d 1 h`
|`9 T 1 h`

|Moment today
|`today 14:02`, `since 14:02`
|`heute 14:02`, `seit 14:02`

|Moment yesterday
|`yesterday 18:40`
|`gestern 18:40`

|Moment within 7 days
|`Mon 18:40`
|`Mo 18:40`

|Moment before
|`12 Sep`
|`12. Sep.`

|Age in a list, under a minute
|`just now`
|`gerade eben`

|Age in a list, under 30 d
|`5 min ago`, `3 h ago`, `9 d ago`
|`vor 5 min`, `vor 3 h`, `vor 9 T`

|Age in a list, 30 d or more
|`last month`, `2 months ago`, `last year`, `3 years ago`
|`letzten Monat`, `vor 2 Monaten`, `letztes Jahr`, `vor 3 Jahren`

* *An age has no week and no yesterday.* An age under 30 days counts minutes, hours, or days, the largest unit that is at least one: three weeks is `21 d ago` and five weeks `last month`, never a count of weeks; a moment of yesterday is `1 d ago` or `20 h ago` as an age, never a bare `yesterday`. _Yesterday_ belongs to the moment (`yesterday 18:40`), not to the age. From 30 days a month is 30 days and a year 365, and their words come from the language's own relative-time data, as the weekday and the month do; the figure counts whole units, so 59 days is `last month`.

* *No ISO string, ever.* No ISO-8601 instant (`2026-10-27T16:18:35Z`), date, or duration (`PT2H10M`) reaches a text, a title, a chip, a hover, or an error message. An ISO string is a transport value: it is read from the server, formatted once, and never shown. The few fields the cleanup contract already ships display-ready — `last_sweep_at`, `next_check_at`, `kept_at`, `ended_at` — are shown as they arrive until they move to an instant, and no new field adds another.
* *One clock.* The age of a plan comes from its start and the age in its phase from the phase start; both views show the same two values, and neither derives one from the other.
* *Words are texts, figures are data.* The words that frame a figure — _today_, _since_, _yesterday_, _ago_, _just now_ — are catalogue texts, so both clients say the same word; the unit marks (`s`, `min`, `h`, and `d`, `T` for the day in German) are fixed in the packs as `k`, `M`, `Tsd.`, and `Mio.` are (<<numbers>>). The weekday, the month, the age in months and years, and the figure itself come from the language's own date and number data, never from a table written by hand.
* *A duration under a minute.* A span shorter than a minute is shown in seconds (`30 s`), as the terminal shows it today; the three duration rows above cover every longer span.
* *Three styles, one rule.* The web app renders a moment in three styles — `relative`, `short`, and `clock`. They are renderings of this rule, not a second format: each takes its words and its figures from it, and no view writes a moment in two of them at once.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Style |Renders |Where

|`relative`
|the distance to now in the unit that distance has — the three rows _Age in a list_ and the _since_ form of _Moment today_
|a list, with the `short` rendering as its hover advisory

|`short`
|the moment itself to the minute — the rows _Moment today_, _Moment yesterday_, _Moment within 7 days_, and _Moment before_, and the time beside the day where a detail shows one (`16 Oct, 11:25`, `16. Okt., 11:25`)
|a detail, and the hover advisory of a list

|`clock`

|the activity stream, with the date on the first row of each day
|===

A row of the activity stream the runtime dates only by its clock belongs to the day the client read it: that day opens the group, and a row the runtime dates not at all opens none, so a day carries one header however its rows arrive.

A duration has one rendering in both clients — `Display.duration` in `pm-api` and `formatDuration` in the web app, as the display layer introduces them — and the three duration rows above are the only forms it uses above a minute.

[#numbers]
== Numbers: Tokens

Every count of tokens is shown in one format, defined here and nowhere else: the plan view, the epic view, the completion report, the system view, and the harness list use it (link:../../../../pm-mcp-core/doc/specification/harness-profiles/01-profile-schema.adoc#token-usage[Tokens, Not Money]). The runtime sends exact counts; the client only formats them, through the keys below, and never adds or derives a figure.

[cols="2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Count |English |German

|below 1 000: the number
|`412`
|`412`

|1 000 to 9 999: thousands with one decimal
|`4.2 k`
|`4,2 Tsd.`

|10 000 to 999 999: whole thousands
|`830 k`
|`830 Tsd.`

|from 1 000 000: millions with two decimals
|`1.24 M`
|`1,24 Mio.`
|===

* *Rounding*: an exact count is rounded half up; a total is rounded from its exact count, never summed from rounded parts. A floor is rounded down at the precision shown, so that it is never overstated: 412 680 measured tokens are `≥ 412 k`, not `≥ 413 k`.
* *The split* takes the unit of its total, so that the parts read against it: `1.24 M (1.10 M in · 0.14 M out)`, `830 k (790 k in · 40 k out)`. _in_ is input, cache-read, and cache-write tokens; _out_ is output and reasoning tokens; the total is their unweighted sum. The split carries no noun: the label beside it says what the figure counts (_tokens so far_, _Tokens_, _Tokens (in · out)_), and the split never repeats that word. Where no label stands beside the figure — the terminal's system view names the calls before it — `common.tokens.total` puts the word in front of the split.
* *A floor*: a figure over records of which some were measured and some not is shown with `≥`, rounded down, and the coverage beside it (`≥ 1.24 M tokens · 3 of 41 tasks not measured`), never as exact; a figure over records of which none was measured is `not measured` with the coverage, never `0` and never a `≥`.
* *Never money*: no price, cost, or currency is shown beside a count of tokens, in any language.

|{value} Tsd.

|`common.tokens.millions`

|{value} Mio.

|`common.tokens.split`
|{total} ({in} in · {out} out)
|{total} ({in} rein · {out} raus)

|`common.tokens.total`
|tokens {figure}

|`common.tokens.at_least`

The client rounds the count as the table says and formats `{value}` with the decimal separator of the interface language (`1.24`, `1,24`); the unit comes from `common.tokens.thousands` or `common.tokens.millions`, never from a formatter's compact notation, so `k`, `M`, `Tsd.`, and `Mio.` are fixed in the packs. A test of `pm-api` formats the table's examples in both languages.
