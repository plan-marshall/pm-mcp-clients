A running plan leads to decisions, small and large, that the operator takes. They are the core interaction of `PM-MCP` with its operator. This document is the channel's side of them: what a client shows of a decision, in which order, and how it is answered, in the terminal and in the web app alike.

[#channel]
== The Channel, Not the Policy

Whether a decision reaches the operator at all, whether a quorum voted on it first and below which confidence it escalates, whether it carries a recommendation, whether the operator may answer in own words, and which role interprets them: all of this is modelled in the plan's workflow units and decision declarations (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/model-work/10-decision-quorum.adoc#decision-declarations[Decision Declarations]). The clients are the channel: they show every variant a unit can produce and answer it with the links the state offers, and they add no rule of their own. Every rule below is about how a client shows what the decision carries, never about what it carries.

The flow is the sub-workflow `decision.*` of link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#UC-PLN-2[UC-PLN-2]; each of its states is rendered like every other state (link:../terminal-client/03-dialogues.adoc#state-frame[The Frame of a State]; link:../web-app/README.adoc#principles[Web App, Principles]).

[#elements]
== What a Decision Carries

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Element |Always |Where the workflow supplies it

|Decision
|id (`<scope_id>/<question_id>`, the id of its first question) and revision, title, header, its subject (a plan, an epic, or a project's setup), the asking phase or setup step and role, what it blocks (the plan, the epic, or the setup, a task, nothing), reversibility (reversible, costly, irreversible), its parts
|description and evidence; a revision after it changed

|Part
|question, answer type (one option, several options, a value), options or the value's bounds
|recommendation with rationale and who recommends it; dependency on options of another part; own words allowed; note allowed

|Option
|key, label, description of its trade-off
|consequences; preview (a diff, a text); support from a quorum: votes for it, out of how many, from which roles and models; dissent with its reason; per vote a self-reported confidence
|===

A client shows an element when the decision carries it and leaves its place out when it does not; it never fills a gap with a guess (no recommendation is shown as "none", not as the first option; no votes is shown as "not voted on", not as zero support).

[#order]
== The Order of a Decision

Both clients show a decision in the same order, top to bottom:

. *What is asked*: the title; its subject (the plan, the epic, or the project's setup), leading to its view: a plan's view, from which its project and, for a plan of an epic, its epic are one step away (link:plan-view.adoc#way-out[Plan View, Its Project and Its Epic]); an epic's view, from which its project is; the project, for its setup, and the phase or step that asks; *why now*: what it blocks; how reversible it is; and that its subject waits for the answer.
. *The evidence*: the description, then the evidence it names (a file and its lines, a diff, a finding, a link). The evidence is what lets the operator decide without trusting the prose; it is one step away (expanded in the web app, `?` in the terminal), never hidden.
. *The parts*, one at a time where there are several (<<parts>>), each with its question and its options in the order the workflow gives them: never sorted by support, so that the order itself recommends nothing.
. *The recommendation and the votes*, where the decision carries them, placed by <<recommendation>>.
. *The answer*: choose; answer in own words where allowed; add a note where allowed; or for the whole decision _chat about it first_ (<<chat>>), _none fits_, or _later_.

A decision is self-contained: whoever opens it hours later finds in it what was asked, why, and what follows, without remembering the plan.

[#support]
== Support, Not Probability

Where a quorum voted, each option shows its support as a count, never as a percentage:

----
  2) Rebase onto main        2 of 3 votes · 2 models    ← recommended
  1) Merge main into branch  1 of 3 votes · 1 model
  3) Stop and ask me later   —
----

* *Count and spread*: votes out of all votes (a quorum has at most three; the operator sees a decision a quorum voted on only when it escalated, so the count shows why it did), and from how many distinct models, because votes of one model, and of models of one maker, are not independent: their errors coincide.
* *Band* beside the counts for the whole part: `unanimous`, `clear majority`, `split`, `no consensus`; always together with the counts, never alone. The counts, the band, and the strongest dissent come from the runtime with the decision; a client never computes them.
* *Dissent*: the strongest reason against the recommended option is shown with it, also when the vote was unanimous (where the quorum gave one): one visible dissent is what keeps a consensus from being taken for proof.
* *Self-reported confidence* of a vote is a detail of that vote (`?` on the vote, expanded in the web app), labelled as the model's own estimate; it is never averaged, never shown as the confidence of an option, and never in decimals.
* *Not voted on*: a part without a quorum shows the recommendation of the asking role as such (`recommended by outline`), without counts.

[#recommendation]
== Where the Recommendation Stands

How prominently the recommendation stands depends on what is at stake, as the decision states it:

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|The decision |The recommendation |`Enter` / the primary button

|reversible, and the votes `unanimous` or a `clear majority`, or a recommendation without a quorum
|shown first, marked, preselected
|takes the recommendation

|reversible, and the votes `split` or `no consensus`
|shown with the options, marked; the dissent beside it
|no preselection: the operator picks

|costly or irreversible
|behind one step: the options first, then the recommendation on request (`?r` in the terminal, a disclosure in the web app)
|no preselection; the answer goes to `decision.confirm`, which restates what follows
|===

A recommendation is written as a recommendation (`Recommended by outline: …`), never as a label suffix, and a long rationale is shortened to its first sentence with the rest one step away: a longer explanation raises acceptance, not correctness.

[#parts]
== Decisions in Parts

* A decision with several parts shows them one at a time, in the order of their dependencies, with a line of where the operator is (`part 2 of 3`) and the answers so far. Parts that depend on nothing may be answered in any order.
* A part that depends on another is shown once that part is answered, with what it depends on (`depends on part 1: Rebase onto main`); changing the earlier answer reopens it, and the client says so.
* `decision.confirm` shows every part with its answer and what follows from it; the operator confirms the whole, or goes back to a part.
* A part may be left for later where it is not required; the decision stays open for it.

[#own-words]
== Answers in Own Words

Where a part allows it, every client offers to answer in own words next to the options; the client offers it, never the workflow as an extra option.

* *Entering*: the words are sent as they are; the client shows that the decision moved to `decision.interpret` and that a model is at work (the interpreting role, by default the shipped role `interpret`, and the time so far: `interpret is reading your answer`; xref:../terminal-client/03-dialogues.adoc#waiting[Waiting]).
* *`decision.read-back`* shows one sentence of what was understood, beside the operator's words with the passages it rests on marked: `I understood: Rebase onto main, provided the release branch stays as it is.` The answers are _confirm_ (the primary one), _edit the words_, and _choose an option instead_. Nothing is recorded before _confirm_.
* *Kinds of result*, each shown as what it is: an option; an option with a condition (the condition named); a new option in the operator's words (`your own answer: …`, recorded as the operator's own option, never as the closest option); a delegation (`you leave it to the plan`, only where the part allows one; the part's recommendation is then taken as the answer, recorded as `delegated`); unclear, which leads to `decision.clarify`. A result the runtime's check rejects (an option that does not exist, a passage not in the words) is shown as unclear.
* *`decision.clarify`* shows one question of the interpreting role that names the readings it could not choose between; the operator's reply is added to the words, which are interpreted again. It asks at most as often as the decision's unit declares (by default twice); once that bound is reached, or the interpreting task ends without a result, the decision returns to `decision.answer` with the part unanswered and the reason shown (`decision.interpret.unclear_bound`), and the operator chooses an option or answers again.
* *Own words disabled*: while no configuration of the interpreting role qualifies, own words are offered disabled with that reason.
* The interpretation never sees the recommendation or the votes, and the client never shows them inside the read-back, so the read-back is about the operator's words alone.

[#chat]
== A Chat Before Answering

At `decision.answer` the operator may ask back before answering (`what does option B mean for the open pull request?`): _Chat about it first_ opens the one chat of both clients (link:work-actions.adoc#chat[Chatting with the Planning Role]) with the decision as its scope. What differs from a chat on a plan is only its scope, its role, and what a proposal does here:

* *Scope*: the decision as this document shows it: the question, why now, the evidence, what it blocks, its reversibility, its parts and options, and the recommendation and the votes, which the chat may explain. The head says `decision Q-007`.
* *Role*: the runtime routes it. A decision a worker asked goes to the role of the task that asked (`Chat · fix-relay · with implement`); one a server rule raised or a quorum escalated goes to the planning role of its subject, `replan` for a plan and `decompose` for an epic. A decision of a project's setup goes to the role of the task that asked; one the setup's workflow raised itself has no planning role, and _Chat_ is offered disabled with that reason (`decision.chat.no_role`, <<setup>>). _Chat_ is disabled with its reason while no configuration of that role qualifies.
* *The chat never answers.* When it ends, the decision is shown again as before, and the operator answers it in any of its ways.
* *A proposed answer* (`Proposed answer, part 2`) is offered as the operator's own words: _Answer in these words_ ends the chat and sends the words to `decision.interpret` unchanged, and the read-back follows as for any words of the operator (<<own-words>>); nothing is recorded before _confirm_, and the interpretation still never sees the recommendation or the votes. A part that takes no own words gets no proposed answer: the chat explains, and the operator chooses.
* *A proposed revision* of the plan (or of the epic's staged plans) is read back and applied as in the chat on a plan, through the guards of the subject's own path; a revision of a plan is proposed only while the plan runs in Phase 5 or in finalize before the merge, so the chat over a decision of an earlier phase (refine, outline) explains and proposes none (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/phase-workflows/08-dynamic-re-planning.adoc#replan-chat[Revision from a Chat]). The decision stays open (`Q-007 stays open; answer it once the chat has ended.`), unless the revision removes the need for it; then the runtime closes it as withdrawn, with the reason, and the client shows that line instead of the decision.
* *The plan waits* during the chat, as for any decision, with no time limit. The transcript is kept with the decision, and its record says that the answer followed a chat (`answered after a chat`).
* *Answered elsewhere* during the chat: the decision closes as in <<outcomes>>, and the chat ends with it (`Q-007 was answered in the web app (iPad); the chat has ended.`); a proposal not yet taken is dropped.
* *A waiver request offers no chat*: the waiver is an operator override, never an answer to its content (<<waiver>>).
* *In the web app* the chat is the panel beside the decision, as beside a plan view; the decision stays visible, its answers disabled while the chat is open.

[#setup]
== A Project's Setup Asks

A decision of a project's workspace scope (a question of its setup, link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/04-projects.adoc#UC-PRJ-1[UC-PRJ-1]) is shown, announced, and answered like a plan's (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#UC-PLN-2[UC-PLN-2]):

* *Its subject* is the setup of the project (`setup of pm-mcp-clients`), with the setup's step that asks in place of a phase; what it blocks is the setup, never a plan. It waits without a time limit, as every decision.
* *Where it is shown*: in the list of open decisions with its project, and on the project's row of Running Work (`setup needs you`, link:plan-overview.adoc#project-row[Plan Overview, A Project]); selecting it opens the decision. The project's setup run shows it where it waits (link:project-onboarding.adoc#setup-decisions[Project Onboarding, When the Setup Asks]).
* *Its chat* goes to the role of the task that asked (an `enrich` task, for example); a question the setup's workflow raised itself has no planning role, and _Chat_ is offered disabled with that reason (`decision.chat.no_role`).
* *Its record* names the project's workspace scope instead of a plan; channel, device, and how it was answered are recorded as for every decision.

[#announce]
== Announcing a Decision

* *Urgency* follows from what the decision carries: it interrupts (a notification, the bell) when it blocks its subject (the plan, the epic, or the setup) or is not reversible; otherwise it waits in the badge, the list of open decisions, and the next notice at a natural break (when a dialogue ends, when the operator returns to the prompt or the overview).
* *Nothing is answered by accident*: an announcement never takes the focus, input typed before it was shown never answers it, and opening a decision discards input typed ahead (xref:../terminal-client/04-console.adoc#answering[Terminal Client, Answering a Question]).
* *No time limit*: a decision delivered to the operator waits until the operator answers it, and the plan waits with it (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/workflow-dsl/02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#operator-wait[Waiting for the Operator]); the clients show how long it has waited, never a countdown, and never answer it on their own. A decision that changed (a new revision) is announced again as changed.
* A notification names its subject (the plan, the epic, or the project's setup: `The setup of pm-mcp-clients asks a decision`) and the kind of decision, never its content (`decision.notify.title`).
* *The terminal client* notifies while it runs (`console.notify`, link:../terminal-client/04-console.adoc[Terminal Client, Console]).
* *The web app* asks once whether it may notify: after the first decision arrives, never at its start, with one line of what it would show; the browser's own prompt follows only on _Allow notifications_. The operator allows or not; without the permission the web app shows the open decisions in the tab title (`(2) Running work · PM-MCP`) and in its badge. The permission is withdrawn in the machine settings (link:../web-app/README.adoc[Web App]), and the web app asks no more once the operator said no.

[#waiver]
== A Waiver Request

A worker that cannot pass a gate may ask for a waiver of it. The request reaches the operator as a decision does, announced the same way (<<announce>>), and waits without a time limit; it is answered by a waiver, which is an operator override and never a content answer.

----
  fix-relay asks for a waiver                                 waiting since 14:02
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Gate          coverage of pm-relay at least 80 %
  Result        76 %: 3 classes without a test
  Asked by      execute · implement
  Its reason    the classes are generated by the annotation processor
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Your reason   [                                                            ]
                                          [Later]   [Decline]   [Waive the gate]
----

* *Three answers*: _Waive the gate_ (`waive`), with the operator's reason; _Decline_ (`decline`), with the operator's reason, which records the outcome `declined` and lets the plan's remediation take its next step; _Later_, which leaves the request open.
* *The reason is required* for both waive and decline; until it is given, both are disabled and say why.
* *No preselection*: neither answer is the primary one, `Enter` in the terminal answers nothing, and no recommendation is shown.
* *No interpretation*: the operator's reason is recorded as it is, never read by a model before it is recorded; there is no answer in own words beside the two, and no chat (<<chat>>).
* The worker's reason and the gate's result may come from a model or the repository and are shown as untrusted text (<<safety>>).

[#outcomes]
== When It Ends Elsewhere

* *Answered in another channel*: every client says where and how (`answered in the web app, iPad: Rebase onto main`), closes the decision if it is open, and drops what was typed into it.
* *A newer revision*: an answer to an older revision is refused by the runtime (`conflict`, with the current revision); the client shows the changed decision with what changed, and the operator answers again. An answer that came after the first valid one is refused `already_answered`, and the client shows where and how the decision was answered; an answer the runtime refuses as invalid (`invalid_answer`) or to a part whose dependency is unanswered (`dependency_unanswered`) keeps the decision open with the reason at its part.
* *None fits* (`decision.withdraw`) is offered only where the decision names a role that asked it, which can ask anew, or where a quorum is declared that can vote again; a decision a server rule raised, a quorum escalated, or the setup asked, with no quorum declared, offers no _none fits_, and the decision stays open until it is answered. Where offered, it is confirmed like an answer, with the note where the operator added one, then shown as `you asked the plan to think again` (the epic, the setup of the project, by its subject; `decision.outcome.rethink`). The runtime closes the decision as withdrawn; the role that asked thinks again and asks anew (or the quorum votes again), as a new decision with an id of its own, announced like any other (<<announce>>) and naming the one it replaces (`asked anew after Q-007`).
* *Later* answers nothing: the decision stays open and announced (`decision.announce`), the client leaves it, and the plan waits; it is in the list of open decisions until it is answered.

[#safety]
== Untrusted Text

Every text of a decision may come from a model or a repository: the question, the description, the evidence, an option, a rationale, a vote. Every client shows it as text only, after removing every control sequence (link:../terminal-client/04-console.adoc#output-safety[Output Safety]; the web app renders no raw HTML), and the operator's own words reach a model only quoted, as data.
