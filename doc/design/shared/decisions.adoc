A running plan leads to decisions, small and large, that the operator takes. They are the core interaction of `PM-MCP` with its operator. This document is the channel's side of them: what a client shows of a decision, in which order, and how it is answered, in the terminal and in the web app alike.

[#channel]
== The Channel, Not the Policy

Whether a decision reaches the operator at all, whether a quorum voted on it first and below which confidence it escalates, whether it carries a recommendation, whether the operator may answer in own words, and which role interprets them: all of this is modelled in the plan's workflow units and decision declarations (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/model-work/10-decision-quorum.adoc#decision-declarations[Decision Declarations]). The clients are the channel: they show every variant a unit can produce and answer it with the links the state offers, and they add no rule of their own. Every rule below is about how a client shows what the decision carries, never about what it carries.

The flow is the sub-workflow `decision.*` of link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#UC-PLN-2[UC-PLN-2]; each of its states is rendered like every other state (link:../terminal-client/03-dialogues.adoc#state-frame[The Frame of a State]; link:../web-app/README.adoc#principles[Web App, Principles]).

[#elements]
== What a Decision Carries

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Element |Always |Where the workflow supplies it

|Decision
|title, header, plan or epic, the asking phase and role, what it blocks (the plan, a task, nothing), reversibility (reversible, costly, irreversible), its parts
|description and evidence; a revision after it changed

|Part
|question, answer type (one option, several options, a value), options or the value's bounds
|recommendation with rationale and who recommends it; dependency on options of another part; own words allowed; note allowed

|Option
|key, label, description of its trade-off
|consequences; preview (a diff, a text); support from a quorum: votes for it, out of how many, from which roles and models; dissent with its reason; per vote a self-reported confidence
|===

A client shows an element when the decision carries it and leaves its place out when it does not; it never fills a gap with a guess (no recommendation is shown as "none", not as the first option; no votes is shown as "not voted on", not as zero support).

[#order]
== The Order of a Decision

Both clients show a decision in the same order, top to bottom:

. *What is asked*: the title; the plan or epic and the phase that asks; *why now*: what it blocks; how reversible it is; and that the plan waits for the answer.
. *The evidence*: the description, then the evidence it names (a file and its lines, a diff, a finding, a link). The evidence is what lets the operator decide without trusting the prose; it is one step away (expanded in the web app, `?` in the terminal), never hidden.
. *The parts*, one at a time where there are several (<<parts>>), each with its question and its options in the order the workflow gives them: never sorted by support, so that the order itself recommends nothing.
. *The recommendation and the votes*, where the decision carries them, placed by <<recommendation>>.
. *The answer*: choose; answer in own words where allowed; add a note where allowed; or for the whole decision _none fits_ or _later_.

A decision is self-contained: whoever opens it hours later finds in it what was asked, why, and what follows, without remembering the plan.

[#support]
== Support, Not Probability

Where a quorum voted, each option shows its support as a count, never as a percentage:

----
  2) Rebase onto main        4 of 6 votes · 3 models    ← recommended
  1) Merge main into branch  2 of 6 votes · 2 models
  3) Stop and ask me later   —
----

* *Count and spread*: votes out of all votes, and from how many distinct models, because votes of one model, and of models of one maker, are not independent: their errors coincide.
* *Band* beside the counts for the whole part: `unanimous`, `clear majority`, `split`, `no consensus`; always together with the counts, never alone.
* *Dissent*: the strongest reason against the recommended option is shown with it, also when the vote was unanimous (where the quorum gave one): one visible dissent is what keeps a consensus from being taken for proof.
* *Self-reported confidence* of a vote is a detail of that vote (`?` on the vote, expanded in the web app), labelled as the model's own estimate; it is never averaged, never shown as the confidence of an option, and never in decimals.
* *Not voted on*: a part without a quorum shows the recommendation of the asking role as such (`recommended by outline`), without counts.

[#recommendation]
== Where the Recommendation Stands

How prominently the recommendation stands depends on what is at stake, as the decision states it:

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|The decision |The recommendation |`Enter` / the primary button

|reversible, and the votes `unanimous` or a `clear majority`, or a recommendation without a quorum
|shown first, marked, preselected
|takes the recommendation

|reversible, and the votes `split` or `no consensus`
|shown with the options, marked; the dissent beside it
|no preselection: the operator picks

|costly or irreversible
|behind one step: the options first, then the recommendation on request (`?r` in the terminal, a disclosure in the web app)
|no preselection; the answer goes to `decision.confirm`, which restates what follows
|===

A recommendation is written as a recommendation (`Recommended by outline: …`), never as a label suffix, and a long rationale is shortened to its first sentence with the rest one step away: a longer explanation raises acceptance, not correctness.

[#parts]
== Decisions in Parts

* A decision with several parts shows them one at a time, in the order of their dependencies, with a line of where the operator is (`part 2 of 3`) and the answers so far. Parts that depend on nothing may be answered in any order.
* A part that depends on another is shown once that part is answered, with what it depends on (`depends on part 1: Rebase onto main`); changing the earlier answer reopens it, and the client says so.
* `decision.confirm` shows every part with its answer and what follows from it; the operator confirms the whole, or goes back to a part.
* A part may be left for later where it is not required; the decision stays open for it.

[#own-words]
== Answers in Own Words

Where a part allows it, every client offers to answer in own words next to the options; the client offers it, never the workflow as an extra option.

* *Entering*: the words are sent as they are; the client shows that the decision moved to `decision.interpret` and that a model is at work (the role, the time so far; xref:../terminal-client/03-dialogues.adoc#waiting[Waiting]).
* *`decision.read-back`* shows one sentence of what was understood, beside the operator's words with the passages it rests on marked: `I understood: Rebase onto main, provided the release branch stays as it is.` The answers are _confirm_ (the primary one), _edit the words_, and _choose an option instead_. Nothing is recorded before _confirm_.
* *Kinds of result*, each shown as what it is: an option; an option with a condition (the condition named); a new option in the operator's words (`your own answer: …`); a delegation (`you leave it to the recommendation: …`, only where the decision allows one); unclear, which leads to `decision.clarify`.
* *`decision.clarify`* shows one question that names the readings it could not choose between, answered in words or by choosing one of them.
* The interpretation never sees the recommendation or the votes, and the client never shows them inside the read-back, so the read-back is about the operator's words alone.

[#announce]
== Announcing a Decision

* *Urgency* follows from what the decision carries: it interrupts (a notification, the bell) when it blocks the plan or is not reversible; otherwise it waits in the badge, the list of open decisions, and the next notice at a natural break (when a dialogue ends, when the operator returns to the prompt or the overview).
* *Nothing is answered by accident*: an announcement never takes the focus, input typed before it was shown never answers it, and opening a decision discards input typed ahead (xref:../terminal-client/04-console.adoc#answering[Terminal Client, Answering a Question]).
* *No time limit*: a decision delivered to the operator waits until the operator answers it, and the plan waits with it (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#change-no-expiry[No Time Limit on a Decision]); the clients show how long it has waited, never a countdown, and never answer it on their own. A decision that changed (a new revision) is announced again as changed.
* A notification names the plan and the kind of decision, never its content.

[#outcomes]
== When It Ends Elsewhere

* *Answered in another channel*: every client says where and how (`answered in the web app, iPad: Rebase onto main`), closes the decision if it is open, and drops what was typed into it.
* *A newer revision*: an answer to an older revision is refused by the runtime; the client shows the changed decision with what changed, and the operator answers again.
* *None fits*: confirmed like an answer, then shown as `you asked the plan to think again`.

[#safety]
== Untrusted Text

Every text of a decision may come from a model or a repository: the question, the description, the evidence, an option, a rationale, a vote. Every client shows it as text only, after removing every control sequence (link:../terminal-client/04-console.adoc#output-safety[Output Safety]; the web app renders no raw HTML), and the operator's own words reach a model only quoted, as data.
