What the overview of running work shows and in which order is link:../shared/plan-overview.adoc[Plan Overview], for both clients (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#UC-PLN-4[UC-PLN-4]). This part is how the console draws it: as a full-screen view for navigating, and as a tree in the scrollback for reading. The conventions of the transcripts are those of xref:05-transcripts-entry.adoc#transcripts[Transcripts].

[#overview-ways]
== Two Ways

* *`plans`* opens the full-screen view: two levels to move through with the keyboard, live, until the operator leaves it (<<full-screen>>).
* *`status`* prints the same content once as a tree into the scrollback: to read, to copy, for a screen reader, and on every terminal the full-screen view cannot use (<<status-tree>>). It is what `plans` prints instead where the full-screen view is not available.
* The prompt's badges keep counting what waits while neither is open (xref:04-console.adoc#prompt[The Prompt]).

[#full-screen]
== The Full-Screen View

[#full-screen-layout]
=== Layout

----
 plan-marshall  Running work · projects                           ● connected
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ! 2  pm-mcp-clients      3 plans · 1 epic                    2 min ago
▶     pm-mcp-core         2 plans                                     9 min ago
      plan-marshall-mcp   1 epic                              1 h ago
 ! 1  plan-marshall       setup needs you
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ↑↓ move   Enter open   ? help   q leave   E ended work   R 5 reports   / filter
----

* *Header*: the full-screen header starts with the product name in bold (`plan-marshall`), then the place (`projects`, or the project and its view), and the connection to the runtime (<<full-screen-brand>>).
* *List*: one row per project, plan, or epic, the selected one marked `▶` and drawn inverted; it scrolls when it is longer than the screen, with `↑ 3 more` and `↓ 5 more` at its edges. Rows follow a single column grid (<<full-screen-grid>>).
* *Footer*: the keys that apply here, filled by priority: `? help` and `q leave` are kept before the
  level's remaining keys, and the line ends with `? more` when it has to leave cells out
  (<<full-screen-footer>>).

* The view opens on the terminal's alternate screen, so the scrollback and the prompt are as they were when it is left.

[#full-screen-brand]
=== The Brand Line

* *Header*: the full-screen header starts with the product name in bold, then the place:
  `plan-marshall  Running work · pm-mcp-clients · by plan                ● connected`.

* *Greeting*: the console greeting starts with `plan-marshall <version> · runtime running`.
* *Prompt*: the prompt stays `pm ›`, which is short and typed often.
* *Mark*: no logo in the terminal. The brand is the name, the colour roles, and the calm layout.

[#full-screen-grid]
=== One Column Grid for Rows

Every row of the overview, the plan view, the reports, and the system view follows one shared column grid at 100 columns or more:

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
|Column |Width |Content

|marker
|2
|`▶` for the selection, else blank

|need
|14
|`! decision`, `! landing` (the kind, cut to 12), else blank

|state
|12
|`▶ running`, `✓ done`, `○ planned`, `‖ paused`

|kind and title
|flexible, 40 at least
|`▸ Fix Relay Transport`, `phase execute`, `TASK-003 implement`

|facts
|rest
|`execute · 2 of 3 tasks · 25 min · #214`, dim except the number that matters
|===

* *Task rows* use the same state column as phase rows.
* *System view*: the system view gets a label column (10) and a sub-label column (12).
* *Truncation*: truncation cuts the title last and the facts first; ids are never cut.

[#full-screen-project]
=== A Project

By plan:

----
 plan-marshall  Running work · pm-mcp-clients · by plan           ● connected
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ! decision  ▸ plan  Console history of answers       refine · waiting for you
                     running 40 min · in refine 12 min · 0 of 0 tasks
▶            ▸ plan  Overview resource of the runtime execute · running
                     running 2 h 10 min · in execute 25 min · 7 of 12 tasks
                     in ◇ client work
             ▸ plan  Message catalogue                outline · pausing after the task
                     running 1 h 5 min · in outline 30 min · 0 of 0 tasks
                     in ◇ client work · off track: repeating
   alongside
 ! landing failed    landing remediation.ci-failure   failed
                     removing 2 moved documents from git   running
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ↑↓ move   Enter open   Esc back   a actions   ? help   q leave   e epic   ! what waits   Tab by epic   E ended work
----

By epic:

----
 plan-marshall  Running work · pm-mcp-clients · by epic           ● connected
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
▶            ◇ epic  client work          2 running · 3 staged
                     running 3 d · 4 of 9 plans
               ├─    ▸ plan  Overview resource of the runtime  execute · running
               └─    ▸ plan  Message catalogue                 outline · pausing after the task
   without epic
 ! decision  ▸ plan  Console history of answers       refine · waiting for you
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ↑↓ move   Enter open   Esc back   a actions   ? help   q leave   ! what waits   Tab by plan   E ended work
----

* Each row is the plan's or epic's first line (kind, title, phase and state) and below it its details (times, progress, the epic it belongs to). On a terminal narrower than 100 columns the details line is left out and `?` on the row shows it; below 60 columns the view does not open and `status` prints instead.
* *The state* of a plan is one of a closed list, the texts `overview.state.*` of link:../shared/plan-overview.adoc#texts[Plan Overview, Texts]: `running`, `pausing` (with its point: `after the task`, `after the phase`), `paused`, `waiting for you`, `held`. A plan's own pull request, where it has one, is shown in its details by its number (`#214`), a terminal hyperlink (OSC 8) to the pull request where the terminal shows one; `?` on the row prints its URL.
* *The marker* `!` names the kind of the first item that needs the operator, the texts `overview.needs.*`: `decision`, `waiver`, `waits for you` (an operator wait), the holds `stalled`, `needs a fix` (a condition), `integrity pause`, `held`, `landing failed`, and `new findings`, `setup`. A paused plan carries no marker for being paused: its state says it.
* *Off track*: a plan one of whose workers the server judges off track shows `off track: <signal>` in its details (`overview.plan.off_track`); it is no marker of what needs the operator and is never counted in one; `Enter` on the row opens the plan view at that task's activity (xref:16-plan-view.adoc#activity[The Plan View, A Worker at Work]).
* *Alongside* (`overview.group.alongside`): below the plans and epics of a project, its running and failed short landings, each with its state, a failed one with the marker `landing failed` and the acts of its hold (xref:19-actions.adoc#holds[What Holds a Plan]), and a running onboarding or setup run of the project, which `Enter` continues (xref:17-projects.adoc#onboarding[Onboarding a Project]); the tree of `status` prints the same group as words (link:../shared/plan-overview.adoc[Plan Overview]).
* `e` on a plan moves to its epic in the view by epic; `Tab` switches between the two views and keeps the selection.
* `Enter` on a plan or an epic opens its own view in the same full-screen view: a plan its plan view (xref:16-plan-view.adoc[The Plan View]); an epic its epic view (link:../shared/epic-view.adoc[Epic View]; drawn as xref:19-actions.adoc#epic-view[An Epic's View] says); `!` leaves the view and opens what waits for the operator in it, as `answer` does (link:../shared/decisions.adoc[Decisions]).
* `a` opens the actions of the selected plan or epic below its row (xref:19-actions.adoc#full-screen[Actions]); `E` opens the project's ended work (<<ended>>).

[#full-screen-keys]
=== Keys

The keys follow the conventions of `k9s`, `lazygit`, and `tig`; they are the same in every language, because they are keys of navigation, not mnemonics of a label.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Key |Action

|`↑` `↓`, `k` `j`
|move the selection

|`PgUp` `PgDn`, `g` `G`
|a page; the first and the last row

|`Enter`, `→`, `l`
|open: a project shows its plans; a plan or an epic opens its own view

|`Esc`, `←`, `h`
|back one level

|`Tab`
|switch the view of a project: by plan, by epic

|`e`
|on a plan: its epic

|`a`
|the actions of the selected plan or epic, below its row (xref:19-actions.adoc#full-screen[Actions])

|`!`
|open what waits for the operator in the selected row

|`E`
|the ended work of the selected project, or of the project shown (<<ended>>)

|`S`
|the system view, as a level of the view (xref:25-system.adoc#full-screen[System, Full Screen])

|`R`
|the unread reports, as a level of the view; the footer of the projects' level shows their count while there are any (xref:24-reports.adoc#full-screen[Reports, In the Full-Screen View])

|`/`
|filter the rows by a part of their name or title; `Esc` clears it

|`r`
|read everything again from the runtime

|`?`
|a box with all keys of the level, grouped, and the details of the selected row (<<full-screen-footer>>)

|`q`
|leave the view to the prompt
|===

[#full-screen-levels]
=== Levels, Roots, and Returning

The full-screen view is one stack of levels; every view of this client is a level of it, and the
keys that open a level are the same everywhere. There are two roots: `projects` (the Explorer,
xref:17-projects.adoc[Projects]) and `plans` (the Overview of this part). A root is the bottom of the
stack and has no level below it.

[cols="2,4,3",options="header"]
|===
|Key / Act |From |Opens

|`plans`, `status --by plan`
|the prompt
|the root `plans` (Overview, by plan)

|`projects`, `Esc` on the `plans` root
|the prompt / the root
|the root `projects` (Explorer); `Esc` on a root stays there

|`Enter`, `→`, `l`
|the `projects` root
|the selected project's level, by plan

|`Enter`, `→`, `l`
|a project, a plan, or an epic level
|the plan view (xref:16-plan-view.adoc[The Plan View]) or the epic view

|`e`
|a plan of the projects level
|its epic, in the view by epic (`Tab` keeps the selection)

|`S`
|every level
|the system level (xref:25-system.adoc#full-screen[System, Full Screen])

|`R`
|every level
|the level of the unread reports (xref:24-reports.adoc#full-screen[Reports, In the Full-Screen View])

|`E`
|the `projects` root or a project level
|the ended work of the selected or shown project (<<ended>>)

|`Esc`, `←`, `h`
|every level but a root
|the level that opened it, with its selection, filter, and scroll position unchanged
|===

* `Esc` pops exactly one level. It first clears an open `/` filter or closes the `?` overlay; only a
  second `Esc` pops. A level opened by `S`, `R`, or `E` returns to the level it was opened from, not
  to a root, so the operator never loses the row they were on.

* `q` leaves the whole view from any level and returns to the prompt.
* The stack belongs to the model, not to the drawing: a level is a record of its kind, its parent,
  and its selection by row id (`TuiViewStack`). The drawing library never owns navigation state.

[#full-screen-key-registry]
=== The Key Registry

Every level declares its bindings in one registry (`TuiKeyRegistry`); the footer is generated from
it, so the footer can never advertise a key the level does not bind, and two actions can never bind
the same key within a level. The registry is the table below, read as: a key applies on the listed
levels and nowhere else.

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|Key |Action (text key) |Levels

|`↑` `↓`, `k` `j`
|move (`operator.overview.keys.move`)
|all

|`PgUp` `PgDn`, `g` `G`
|page, first/last
|all

|`Enter`, `→`, `l`
|open (`operator.overview.keys.open`)
|all but a leaf

|`Esc`, `←`, `h`
|back (`operator.overview.keys.back`)
|all but the filter/help overlay, which consume the first `Esc`

|`Tab`
|switch view (`operator.overview.keys.switch_view`)
|project levels (`by plan` / `by epic`)

|`e`
|epic (`operator.overview.keys.epic`)
|plan rows of the projects level

|`a`
|actions (`operator.overview.keys.actions`)
|plan and epic rows

|`!`
|what waits (`operator.overview.keys.waits`)
|rows with a `!` marker

|`E`
|ended work (`operator.overview.keys.ended`)
|`projects` root and project levels

|`S`
|system (`operator.overview.keys.system`)
|all

|`R`
|reports (`operator.overview.keys.reports`)
|all

|`/`
|filter (`operator.overview.keys.filter`)
|all

|`r`
|reload (`operator.overview.keys.reload`)
|all

|`?`
|help and row details (`operator.overview.keys.help`)
|all

|`q`
|leave (`operator.overview.keys.leave`)
|all
|===

A key the registry does not bind on a level is not drawn in that level's footer. The guard test
`TuiKeyRegistryTest` fails for a footer key without a binding and for two bindings of one key on one
level.

[#full-screen-footer]
=== The Footer and the Help Overlay

* *Priority*: each binding carries a priority in the registry. The footer keeps its cells in that
  order — move first, then open, back, the level's own act on its row, `? help` and `q leave`, then
  the rest in the order the level declares them — so a narrow terminal drops the tail of the line
  and never a key that matters more.

* *Fill*: the footer fills the terminal's width and never cuts a key in the middle. When the next
  cell does not fit whole, the line stops there and ends with `? more`, the same key that opens the
  overlay which shows every binding the width left out.

* *The overlay*: `?` draws a box over the view with the level's bindings, grouped as move, open,
  actions, views and leave, and the details of the selected row; a narrow box wraps a group's keys
  under them, and a body too short for all of it drops from the end: the groups first, then the
  details. While it is open the footer shows `Esc close`; the first `Esc` closes the box and leaves
  the level where it was, and only a second `Esc` goes back one level.

[source,text]
----
┌ keys ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ move     ↑ k   ↓ j   PgUp   PgDn   g   G                                    │
│ open     Enter → l   ← h                                                    │
│ actions  *   .   n   t   p   c   a   /   r                                  │
│ views    e   ?                                                              │
│ leave    Esc   q                                                            │
│                                                                             │
│     ▶ TASK-003 · implement · claude sonnet · attempt 1 · running · - · 6 m… │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
----

[#full-screen-narrow]
=== Narrow Terminals

The same thresholds hold in every view of the terminal:

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Width |What is drawn

|`>= 100` columns
|the full layout: header, row line, and the details line below each row

|`>= 80` and `< 100`
|row line only; the details line is left out, and `?` on the row prints it

|`>= 60` and `< 80`
|row line only, truncated to the width; the footer keeps the keys that apply, by priority, and ends
with `? more` when it does not hold them all (<<full-screen-footer>>)

|`< 60` columns
|the view does not open: the command names the reason (`operator.tui.unavailable.too_narrow`) and
 then prints the tree as `status` does (<<status-tree>>)
|===

* Every cell is truncated to the terminal's column width without splitting a surrogate pair or a
  wide glyph; a truncated cell ends in `…` (`U+2026`). A cell's display width is its number of
  columns, not its number of `char`s.

* `?` shows the row's details as the overlay has room for them, whatever the width: the same text
  the details line would show on a wide terminal, cut to the box's width, and its tail left out of a
  body too short to hold it.

* A window resize (`WINCH`) redraws at the new width; crossing a threshold changes the shape of the
  screen as the table says, without leaving the level.

[#full-screen-console-handoff]
=== The Hand-Off from the Console

`plans` and its alias `tui` typed at the console's prompt open the same view as the direct command,
on the terminal the console already owns
(xref:04-console.adoc#full-screen-handoff[The Console, The Full-Screen Hand-Off]).

* *One process has one terminal.* The console creates it on entry; a full-screen verb it dispatches
  draws on that terminal and never opens a second one. JLine's first system terminal owns the
  process terminal, so a second `TerminalBuilder` would fall back to a dumb terminal with no size,
  and the view would refuse without saying why.

* The console brackets the dispatch: notices are held while the view is open, and one meanwhile line
  is printed after `q` (<<full-screen-live>>). A notice never corrupts the alternate screen.

* `--json` runs without the view and without the notice hold; stdout stays JSON.
* After `q` the console redraws its prompt and remains usable; `exit` then leaves with code `0`.
* A full-screen verb the console cannot open prints the refusal line
  (<<full-screen-not-available>>) and its scrollback output, and the prompt returns.

[#full-screen-not-available]
=== Why the View Does Not Open

The same verdict decides the greeting's hint, the console hand-off, and the refusal. The reasons are
checked in this order, and the first applicable one is named (`FullScreenAvailability.Reason`):

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Reason |When |Text key

|`NON_INTERACTIVE`
|The process is not attached to an interactive terminal
|`operator.tui.unavailable.non_interactive`

|`DUMB_TERMINAL`
|The terminal's type is `dumb` or `dumb-color`
|`operator.tui.unavailable.dumb_terminal`

|`CONFIGURED_OFF`
|`console.full_screen` of `machine-config.json` is `false`
|`operator.tui.unavailable.configured_off`

|`TOO_NARROW`
|The terminal is narrower than the view's minimum (60 columns)
|`operator.tui.unavailable.too_narrow`
|===

* A requested `plans`, `plan`, `projects`, or `system` prints one catalogue line naming the reason
  to stderr, then the scrollback it would otherwise print, and exits `0`. Under `--json` it prints no
  reason; stdout stays the JSON.

* The greeting's hint (`operator.console.hint.tui`) is printed only when the verdict is available,
  so it never names a verb that falls back.

* `plans` below 60 columns refuses before it draws: no alternate screen is entered, the reason is
  named, and the tree is printed as `status` does.

* A `dumb` terminal or `console.full_screen=false` is a verdict of the terminal or the machine
  configuration, not a broken view; the tree is complete.

[#full-screen-connection]
=== The Header's Connection State

The header's right side is the connection to the runtime:

* `● connected` (`operator.overview.connected`) while the event stream is open.
* `○ reconnecting` (`operator.console.badge.reconnecting`) while it is not; the last snapshot stays
  on screen and no row is removed for being unreachable.

* On reconnect the client reads the whole snapshot again (the open levels' pages, the needs
  counters, and the reports count) and redraws once; it never replays the missed events as notices,
  because a restarted runtime holds no events to replay. The selection stays on its row id, or on
  the nearest row when the id is gone.

* `q` while disconnected leaves the view as usual; the console then prints the reconnecting state at
  its prompt (xref:04-console.adoc#connection[The Console, Connection]).

[#full-screen-dialogue]
=== Handing Off to a Dialogue

A dialogue is line-oriented and owns the terminal: the full-screen view leaves the alternate screen
before it starts a `LineReader` dialogue, and re-enters it when the dialogue returns.

* The view first draws the cursor normally and leaves the alternate screen
  (`exit_ca_mode`), so the dialogue runs on the ordinary scrollback as every dialogue does.

* When the dialogue returns, the view enters the alternate screen again, reads a fresh snapshot
  (because the dialogue may have changed what it shows), and redraws the same level with the same
  selection.

* A notice that arrives while the dialogue runs is printed by the dialogue's own notice handling,
  not held by the view; the view's notice buffer is inactive while it is suspended.

* An exception in the dialogue is the dialogue's to report; the view still restores the terminal in
  its `finally` and re-enters the alternate screen only on a normal return.

[#full-screen-live]
=== Live, and Leaving

* The view follows the runtime's events and redraws only what changed; the selection stays on the selected item, wherever its row moves. A notice that would be printed above the prompt is held while the view is open.
* *Leaving* (`q`) restores the screen the operator left and prints one line of what arrived meanwhile, then the prompt. The line names the place the operator was in — `the overview`, or the header of the level that was left — and it is printed whether or not something arrived: nothing arrived is said as well, so the operator is told where they were and that nothing waits.

[source,text]
----
pm › plans
  (the full-screen view, until q)
While the overview was open: 1 decision arrived (Q-014, plan fix-relay), 2 new reports (reports).
pm [1 question] ›
----

Leaving a view where nothing arrived reads the same sentence with the other event:

[source,text]
----
pm › plans
  (the full-screen view, until q)
While the overview was open: nothing arrived.
pm ›
----

[#full-screen-build]
=== How It Is Built

* On JLine 4 alone, without another library: `Display` draws the screen and updates only the cells that changed, `BindingReader` and `KeyMap` read the keys, the terminal's capabilities switch to the alternate screen, hide the cursor, and set the keypad mode; everything is restored in a `finally`, also on an error, and the terminal's resize signal redraws. JLine's own `ttop` is built the same way.
* The view model (the rows, the selection by id, the filter, the actions of the keys) is kept apart from the drawing, so that the drawing can change its library later without the model.
* One thread draws: the events of the runtime only update the model and mark it changed, and the view's loop redraws, waiting for a key with a short timeout.
* `console.full_screen` of `machine-config.json` (link:../../../../pm-mcp-core/doc/specification/store-schemas/12-machine-stores.adoc#machine-config[Machine Configuration Store]) chooses whether the views open full screen: `true` where the terminal allows it; `false`, and `plans` prints the tree as `status` does.

[#overview-persistence]
=== Where the Operator Left It

The view stores the project, the view of the project, and the id of the selected plan or epic in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/pm-operator/overview.json` (default `~/.local/state/pm-operator/overview.json`), when the operator leaves it; the next `plans` opens there, or falls back as link:../shared/plan-overview.adoc#persistence[Where the Operator Left It] says. Like the history, the file lies outside `<PM_MCP_BASE>` and holds no content of a plan.

[#status-tree]
== The Tree (`status`)

[source,text]
----
pm › status
Runtime pm-mcpd 0.9.1, running since 08:14 · 2 workers · 1 of 2 build slots in use

! 2  pm-mcp-clients · 3 plans · 1 epic
     ! decision  plan  Console history of answers   refine · waiting for you · 40 min
                 plan  Overview resource of the runtime   execute · running · 2 h 10 min
                       in epic client work · 7 of 12 tasks
                 plan  Message catalogue   outline · pausing after the task · 1 h 5 min
                       in epic client work
     pm-mcp-core · 2 plans
                 plan  Session log and generations   execute · running · 4 h
                       pull request #198 https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/pull/198
                 plan  Profile loader   finalize · paused · 6 h
! 1  plan-marshall · setup needs you
pm [1 question] ›
----

* The same rows, in the same order, as words: the kind is a word (`plan`, `epic`), the state is a word of the closed list (`running`, `pausing`, `paused`, `waiting for you`, `held`), the marker `!` is followed by its kind, and no meaning is in a colour or a glyph alone.
* A plan's own pull request is printed by its number and its URL, so that it can be opened from the scrollback.
* By default the tree is the view by plan: plans flat, each naming its epic. `status --by epic` prints the view by epic: the epics with their running and staged plans, and their plans below them; `status --project <id>` prints one project; `status --ended` prints the ended work (<<ended>>).
* The tree is printed once; it is the view of a screen reader, of a terminal without cursor control, and of a narrow one.

[#ended]
== Ended Work

What ended work lists is link:../shared/plan-overview.adoc#ended[Plan Overview, Ended Work] (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/use-cases/03-plans.adoc#UC-PLN-4[UC-PLN-4], its variation). The terminal reaches it two ways:

* *`E`* in the full-screen view opens the ended work of the selected project (on the level of the projects) or of the project shown, as a level of the same view; `Esc` goes back to where it was opened.
* *`status --ended [--project <project_id>]`* prints it into the scrollback; without `--project`, the project of the working directory, else every project with ended work, newest first.

----

            the approach is replaced by plan relay-v2
  ◇ epic  client bootstrap                  completed   2 weeks ago
  ▸ plan  Message catalogue v0              abandoned   3 weeks ago
            superseded by the shared texts
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ↑↓ move   Enter open   Esc back   a actions   ? help   q leave   / filter
----

* Each row is the plan or epic with its outcome (a plan: `merged`, `no change`, `handed back`, `returned`, `cancelled`, `abandoned`, `released`, `aborted`; an epic: `completed`, `cancelled`, `released`; link:../shared/work-actions.adoc#outcomes[How a Plan Ends]), its reason where it has one, and when it ended; newest first; the client reads the runtime's pages as the operator scrolls (full screen) or as it prints (`status --ended`).
* `Enter` on an ended plan opens its plan view as it ended (xref:16-plan-view.adoc#ended[An Ended Plan]), on an ended epic its epic view as it ended (link:../shared/epic-view.adoc[Epic View]); both are read-only: no live updates, and no action but _Chat…_, which `a` offers with `fix-relay has ended: its view, and a chat about it` (xref:19-actions.adoc#chat-ended[A Chat About Ended Work]). `a` on a row of ended work opens the same menu. `plan <plan_id>` and `epic <epic_id>` print an ended plan or epic the same way, with `ended` in its first line.
